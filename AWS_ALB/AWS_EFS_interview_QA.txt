Real-Time Interview Questions and Answers
Scenario 1
Question

Your company website receives heavy traffic during a sale event. One EC2 instance becomes overloaded. What scaling method would you recommend?

Answer

Step 1:
Identify whether the issue is resource limitation or traffic growth.

Step 2:
For temporary resource shortage:

Use Vertical Scaling.

Step 3:
For long-term traffic growth:

Use Horizontal Scaling.

Step 4:
Deploy multiple instances behind ALB.

Step 5:
Use Auto Scaling to automatically add servers.

Result:
Application remains highly available during peak traffic.

Scenario 2
Question

One EC2 instance fails unexpectedly. How does ALB handle the situation?

Answer

Step 1:
ALB performs health checks.

Step 2:
Instance fails health check.

Step 3:
Unhealthy threshold is reached.

Step 4:
ALB marks instance as unhealthy.

Step 5:
Traffic is redirected to healthy instances.

Result:
No downtime for users.

Scenario 3
Question

Your organization hosts a website and APIs on the same domain. How can ALB help?

Answer

Step 1:
Create Target Group for Website.

Step 2:
Create Target Group for APIs.

Step 3:
Configure Path-Based Routing.

Example:

/app/*  → TG-Web
/api/*  → TG-API

Step 4:
ALB routes traffic automatically.

Result:
Single Load Balancer manages multiple applications.

Scenario 4
Question

Why should web servers allow traffic only from the ALB Security Group?

Answer

Step 1:
Users access ALB only.

Step 2:
ALB forwards traffic to EC2 instances.

Step 3:
Direct public access to EC2 is blocked.

Benefits:

Improved Security
Reduced Attack Surface
Better Network Control
Scenario 5
Question

A health check is configured every 10 seconds. The server fails two consecutive checks. What happens?

Answer

Step 1:
ALB detects failures.

Step 2:
Unhealthy threshold reaches 2.

Step 3:
Instance becomes unhealthy.

Step 4:
Traffic is stopped to that instance.

Step 5:
Only healthy instances receive requests.
